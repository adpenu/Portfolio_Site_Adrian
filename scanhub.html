Scanhub


Dentistry has rapidly embraced digital innovations, transforming traditional practices into highly efficient, precise, and technology-driven solutions within just a few decades.

Dental labs need to adapt to the growing digital demands of clinics especially in managing the increase of digital orders.







CASE STUDY

End results achieved

  • Feature that unifies incoming dental scans from different platforms

  • Notification system that allows lab admins to book in orders within seconds of being received

  • Significantly reducing time allocated for order bookings

  • Removal of repetitive work using AI mapping of dentist, practice and product selections

My role

As a UX Designer, my role was to create user-centric solutions to share with stakeholders and test with users.

I was also responsible for designing the UI following our design library guidelines.


I worked cross-functionally with product manager,engineers and customer support.



EMPATHISING WITH THE USER

Defining the issue

Dentists send digital orders to labs together with intraoral scan attachments. Depending on the type of scanner used, dentists will upload these cases via different platforms. Dental technicians will have to check each platform for updates which can be time consuming and error-prone.


We believe dental labs need a platform to unify incoming digital orders so that they can improve booking speed and customer satisfaction.

Outlining the solution

A centralised system that reads new uploads and redirects them to a hub, called Scanhub.

My motivation

Having worked in a dental lab and experienced the frustrations of our users I understood very well the positive impact a feature like this can have.


I was motivated to design Scanhub with the user needs in mind.



IDEATION

Building the framework

Using our hypothesis I began creating user stories for all possible pain points and match them with the functional requirements established by stakeholders. Working this way helps me flesh out the basic functionality of the feature whilst taking both user needs and business requirements into consideration.

Main challenge

Together with the discovery team we conducted user interviews in order to confirm our hypothesis. I've also created preliminary designs to get user feedback. During these meetings we understood that we have to refine the system so that it automatically matches scan details with existing data (doctor name, product name, etc.)



THE FRAMEWORK

Setting the design direction

Sketching and prototyping helped with finding the arrangment of elements and to define the anatomy of the feature. We took inspiration from Xero's payment reconciliation page. This was a good example to start with as we needed a clear and simple way of showing users both groups of data (incoming scan details vs. order details).

Structuring the experience

Once the stakeholders agreed on this approach, I started creating user journeys based on scenarios and plot-points to conceptualise the pathways and decision trees user would face. This gave us the opprotunity to understand how the interface will support the user in any given situation.



DETAILED DESIGN

Prototyping & Iterations

Scanhub is a major selling point for GreatLab. Not being developed at that time meant that the Figma prototype was the only way of showcasing the feature to clients. Also the developers had to start working on the backend and infrastructure so that led to fast prototyping.



THE REFINEMENT

Deeper insights

We launched the MVP of Scanhub to selected clients. We selected labs that have at least 60% of digital orders with a majority from 3Shape. I worked closely with Sales, Customer support and stakeholders to organise user interviews and take part in client calls.

Users were having problems setting up rules for automations so we needed to make the system learn their preferences and suggest automations to create.

Automating the process

To make the booking process faster and less repetitive, we've offered users the ability to automate the flow. The user needs to create rules based on conditions and the sytem will recognise them once they're met.
Taking it a step further, everytime user matches incoming data with existing data, Scanhub will offer users to create an automation for it so next time it will be applied instantly.

We decided to do this after observing users having issues with integrating automations and not knowing where to start.

Using Scanhub in a team

The issue of having multiple lab admins booking in cases and the (slim) possibility of duplicating an order. The solution was to add an allocation system which means that users are allocated cases when they want to start booking.
Also an animation was added to the flow which offers enough buffer time to avoid multiple admins starting the same case at the same time.

Creating accounts on-the-go

Users were questioning how new clients will be registered in the system if the first point of contact is Scanhub. We had to give the ability to create doctor and patient accounts without having to leave the page.
I've added the functionality of creating new accounts from the search bar.

Improving the UI

To enhance readability I've redesigned the buttons so they show only relevant icons and reveal tooltip description on hover. This enabled the cards to expand horizontally thus creating more white space and reducing cognitive load.



THE IMPACT

Positive results and much more to do

At the time of writing, 3Shape was the only provider that we integrated with other big names in the pipeline (iTero, Medit etc.). Because of this not all labs have started using Scanhub yet and are relying on previous methods to track digital orders.


The labs that have adopted Scanhub as part of their workflow have given positive feedback and we aknowledge that there's still a lot to learn and improve on the capability of this feature.

Due to the industry-specific nature of the software and with new clients joining every week the time spent on training and subsequent costs had to be kept in check. The balancing act between learnability and efficiency had to be achieved.


The steepness of the learning curve and the efficiency of interaction would indicate how high is the productivity that users can reach once they've fully learned how to use the product. Implementing shortcuts seemed the most viable solution for carefully balancing learnability and efficiency.



Learning curve and the efficiency of shortcuts in the ordering flow.


Dentists have their own preferences and specific way of working so we decided to compile a 'best practices' report for each client and use it as a shortcut during the ordering phase. This will save them time by avoiding repetititve actions. Alternatively they will have the option of customising a case if they wish.



The progress bar feature was designed to offer more context by letting the users know where they are in completing a certain action. It also helps with learnability and memorability. We decided to try and integrate it in all our flows as a form of gamification and to minimize cognitive load.


Vertical progress bar informing user about the current status of the task and the steps needed to completion.




Horizontal progress bar for order tracking.

Another concern was to offer users the possibility to place different type of products within the same order. Currently the software allows dentists to checkout multiple products but only if they're the same type of restoration and made of the same material (ie. metal, zirconia, etc.).

"Dentists should be able to order crowns, bridges, implants or veneers for the same patient in one go. Similar versatility to a food delivery app."


Setting the design direction

Taking a top-down approach to defining the overall framework of the experience required higher emphasis on planning and understanding how the new features will affect the system as a whole.


I generated stacks of ideas about the arrangement of UI, functional and data elements, and interactive behaviours. Starting broad, our vision began evolving into something tangible. A high‐level design language, interactions and minimalistic composition began to piece together.

Early sketches exploring different navigation patterns for the order flow.

Because of time pressure, I worked rapidly often jumping straight from sketching to prototyping.

I've created site maps to share with stakeholders and engineers with the purpose of establishing a common understanding of how the ordering process will look like.


Before moving to the design stage the developers suggested to map out the ordering system in its entirety so they can see how everything will fit in together. Thus I continued the site mapping for implant and restorative systems.


"Working together with the engineers using an Agile methodology allowed for adaptive planning and better time management."

The next hurddle was to turn these highly technical road maps into user friendly layouts that increase efficiency and improve navigation. Creating the blueprint allowed us to find patterns that can help devise workable solutions around every single piece of functionalty.



The interaction experience

Understanding the usage contexts of the platform helped me develop a clear vision of the tonal expectations of our users. To communicate the personality of our app to our client and team, we developed a set of experience principles. These were used to sense‐check design decisions, articulate core values and describe key attributes the product should uphold for both the users and the brand. The principles were used constantly to drive the aesthetic, feel and overall tonal direction of the app.

Mid-fidelity wireframes for clear aligners order flow. Example of layout with progress bar integration and use of dental chart (right image).

For the crown & bridge order flow I've used collapsable menu customised for each product allowing dentists to adjust the experience for different requirements and minimizing cognitive load.

Mobile version

INSIGHTS

Reflections

These changes have improved the quality of service and there has been an increase in orders in the final quarter of 2022. The product that was in place before I joined the team was unidimensional and rigid. By demanding high standards from ourselves and the work produced we want to create a culture that seeks to earn trust through accountability, attention to detail and continuous improvement.


The approach of the company is for incremental developement of the SaaS platform based on research and feasibility. Besides delivering high quality dental restorations the company has to equally focus on the digital product that it's offering to customers. This challenge is not something that traditional dental labs have to deal with and is revolutionising the industry.


What's next

Improving the navigation and interface elements are an ongoing process. We learn from feedback and usability testing so we can further reiterate and minimise frictions. Now that we have a solid framework to build upon we can focus on developing the restorative and implant based systems on the platform. A unified internal system for communication between technicians and dentists is also in the works.
